The routes of this page go into every profile an app takes whole: **Clash apps on mihomo** (Koala Clash, Clash Verge, FlClash, ClashFest, SlothClash and others), sing-box apps that take a whole config, and Happ and INCY with Xray configs on. Each core writes them in its own terms, and what it cannot say as it is goes in the nearest form. Apps that get a list of links (v2RayTun, v2rayNG, Hiddify, Karing and others) don't get these routes. The names of the settings and the syntax of the rules below are Clash's.

## Mode

**Settings → Routing → Routing in Clash apps**:

- **Russian sites direct** (default): Russian sites bypass the VPN based on geodata.
- **Everything through the VPN**.
- **Only what is blocked**: sites from Russian blocklists and services you sent to the VPN yourself go through the tunnel. Everything else goes direct.

## Services

For each service you can choose: "Like the rest" (follows the mode), "Through the VPN", "Direct", "Block" or "Through a server", meaning a specific node. The services: ads and trackers, YouTube, Telegram, Discord, WhatsApp and Signal, Instagram and Facebook, TikTok, X (Twitter), ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, Netflix and Spotify, Twitch, games (Steam, Epic, Riot…), GitHub, torrents.

**Past the VPN**: Russian apps and sites (banks, government services, marketplaces) bypass the tunnel. On Android and desktop this works by app names, on iPhone by sites.

## DNS

Empty: the panel's default DNS. Ready sets: "The panel's", "All through the VPN", "Russian domains by Yandex". Servers are written as in mihomo: an IP or `https://`, `tls://`, `quic://`, and `#PROXY` at the end sends the query through the VPN. You can also set DNS for the servers' addresses and your own DNS for domains, one per line like `+.cn: https://dns.alidns.com/dns-query`.

The **Show the profile** button shows what the app will get.

## DNS at the exit

**Settings → Routing → DNS at the exit.** On from the first start, on every node.

Some apps (Happ, v2RayTun, v2rayNG and others on the Xray core) look a site's address up on the device and send the node a ready IP. A site may then decide the user is in their own country and say "not available in your region". With this setting the node finds out which site the user goes to and connects to it from its own country. It does not get in the way of mihomo (Clash) clients: they send the node the site's name anyway.

- **Find out the site on the server**: the node reads the site's name from the connection (TLS, HTTP and QUIC on ports 443, 8443, 80 and 8080–8880). Other ports are left alone.
- **DNS server**: "The server's own" fits almost always. There are presets for Cloudflare, Google and Quad9 (over DoH) and an own list: up to 8 servers, for example `1.1.1.1`, `tls://dns.google`, `https://1.1.1.1/dns-query`, `quic://dns.adguard-dns.com`.

Nodes older than 0.5.2.0 skip this setting; after `mikan update` it works by itself.

## Your own Clash profile

:::note[New in 0.5.2.1]
The templates replace the one own profile of earlier versions: it became the default Clash template, and sing-box and Xray got templates of their own.
:::

A whole mihomo config of your own (DNS, fake-ip, TUN, sniffer, groups, rules) is a subscription template in **Settings → Subscription → Templates**. While Clash has a default template, the settings of this page don't reach Clash apps, and the **Routing** section says so at the top. How to write templates and how response rules pick them: [Subscription templates](/mikan/en/subscriptions/templates/).

## Custom rules

**Settings → Clash rules**: one rule per line, `TYPE,VALUE,TARGET[,no-resolve]`, lines with `#` are comments. Rules go before the routing mode in every Clash profile, while the addresses of the panel and nodes still go direct.

```text
DOMAIN-SUFFIX,example.ru,DIRECT
DOMAIN-SUFFIX,openai.com,PROXY
GEOSITE,category-ads-all,REJECT
PROCESS-NAME,Telegram.exe,PROXY
IP-CIDR,192.168.0.0/16,DIRECT,no-resolve
```

- **Types**: DOMAIN, DOMAIN-SUFFIX, DOMAIN-KEYWORD, GEOSITE, GEOIP, IP-CIDR, IP-CIDR6, IP-SUFFIX, SRC-IP-CIDR, DST-PORT, SRC-PORT, NETWORK, PROCESS-NAME, PROCESS-PATH. DOMAIN-REGEX, IP-ASN, SRC-GEOIP and DOMAIN-WILDCARD are received only by apps with a recent core.
- **Targets**: DIRECT, REJECT, REJECT-DROP, PROXY (the main group), the name of the main group or of the auto-select group.
- Every line is checked on save, and an error names the line number, so a typo won't break clients' profiles. Up to 500 rules.

The example buttons add common rules in one click.

## Torrent blocking

On the same tab: **Block torrents**. Nodes look at the first bytes of a connection and recognize the BitTorrent handshake, DHT, uTP and trackers. Encrypted BitTorrent may slip through.

**On a catch**: only drop the connection, or ban the user for a chosen time. The ban applies to the user, not the IP, and takes effect on all nodes at once. A single tracker request bans nobody; it takes three within ten minutes, because a web page can make a browser send such a request.

In the user's card you can lift a ban or turn on "Do not check for torrents". Those caught are listed under "Caught", and a "Torrent caught" notification arrives in Telegram.
