A group decides what its clients see. It may have servers of its own instead of the plan's, and addresses for clients can be given out by group. For example, a "Mobile RU" group sees only two protocols and an address behind a CDN, while "VIP" sees every server of the plan and one more address.

Groups are not folders: a folder only keeps the list in order and changes nothing, while a group changes the subscription and where the nodes let a client in.

## Which group a client is in

A client is in one group at most:

1. **Its own group** is set on the client's card: the Group block → Change.
2. Without one, the client is in its **plan's group**. It is set in the plan's form, under "What is included" → **"Client group"**. This way the plan's buyers and trial users go to the group on their own, with nobody moving them.
3. With neither, the client is in no group and sees its plan's servers.

"As in the plan" in the card's menu takes its own group off: the client goes back to the plan's group. Its own group wins over the plan's, and the card says so.

Many clients change group at once by a bulk action: tick them in the list, **To group**, pick the group. The panel first shows how many it will change. "As in the plan" takes the own groups off every client ticked.

## Groups and access

**Clients → Groups** opens the list of groups. A group has a name, an icon and a colour, as a folder does, and **servers**:

- **As in the client's plan**: the group leaves the servers alone, the client sees its plan's.
- **Its own**: the nodes ticked, whole (with the protocols added to them later), and single protocols. They **replace** the plan's servers for every client of the group.

The order is: the client's own list of protocols on its card, then the group's own servers, then the plan's servers, and without a plan every server. The card says under More settings → Protocols where the client's servers come from.

A group's servers apply at once, and not only to the subscription: the nodes let the client in on them alone. Server groups and their limits work as before: a server group closed for a client stays closed in a client group with servers of its own too. Traffic packages and the gifts of a plan are checked against the servers the plan's clients stand on: the servers of the plan's group, when it has its own.

What a given client gets is checked on its card, in the **What the client gets** block: it shows the client's group, the servers of its subscription and everything left out of it, with why. More: [what the client gets](/mikan/en/subscriptions/clients/#what-the-client-gets).

## Addresses by group

Every address for clients (**Protocols** → Configure → Addresses for clients) has a **Groups** field: everyone, the chosen groups only, or everyone but them. More on addresses: [several addresses for clients](/mikan/en/protocols/behind-proxy/).

In the list of groups, editing a group shows which addresses are given out by it and how: "groups only" or "all but this group".

## Deleting

Deleting a group deletes neither clients nor plans: the clients go back to their plans' servers, the plans stay without a group. An address given to "everyone but" this group alone is given to everyone again. An address for "the chosen groups only" left with no group is given to nobody until other groups are picked for it. The delete dialog says how many such addresses there are.

## API

- `GET`, `POST /api/v1/groups`; `PATCH`, `DELETE /api/v1/groups/{id}`; `PUT /api/v1/groups/order`. A group's servers are its `servers` field: `{"as_tariff": false, "nodes": [...], "inbounds": [...]}`.
- A client has `group_id` (its own group) and `effective_group_id` (the group it is in now: its own or its plan's). `PATCH /api/v1/users/{id}` with `group_id` sets its own group, `0` takes it off.
- A plan has `group_id`: the group of its clients without one of their own. In `PUT /api/v1/tariffs/{id}`, `0` takes it off and a field left out keeps it.
- The client list filters by the `group` parameter (`all`, `none` or a group's id) and counts clients by group in `group_counts`. The bulk action `group` with `group_id` gives clients their own group, and without `group_id` takes it off.
- An address for clients has `group_mode` (`only`, `never` or empty) and `groups` (the groups' ids).
